How to choose school ERP software: a 12-point checklist

Twelve questions to ask any school ERP vendor before you sign — from data ownership and real yearly cost to what happens on exam day.

A school ERP touches everyone: the office, every teacher, and every parent. Choosing the wrong one costs a year of frustration. These twelve questions work for any vendor — including us.

Daily use

  1. Can a teacher take attendance in under a minute, on a phone? Ask the vendor to do it live, in front of you, on an ordinary phone.
  2. Does it follow your fee structure as settings? Instalments, concessions, sibling discounts and late fines should be configured, not custom-coded.
  3. Can it print report cards in your board's format? Bring last year's report card and ask to see it reproduced.
  4. Will parents actually use the app? Install it yourself. If it is slow or confusing, parents will go back to WhatsApp groups.

Your data

  1. Who owns the data — and can you export all of it to Excel, any time? Get the answer in the contract.
  2. How is it backed up? Ask how often, where, and when they last tested restoring a backup.
  3. Does every user see only what their role allows? A class teacher should not see another class's fee records.

Money and setup

  1. What will it really cost each year? Ask for a written three-year price: per-student fee, setup, SMS and WhatsApp charges, payment-gateway fees and yearly increases.
  2. Who imports your data and trains your staff, and how long does it take? "You can upload it yourself" usually means weeks of office work.
  3. What support do you get? Phone or WhatsApp, a named person, and response times in writing.

Proof

  1. What happens on the busiest day? Results week, when every teacher enters marks at once, is when weak systems slow down.
  2. Can you speak to two schools already using it? A good vendor will happily connect you.

Red flags

  • A large upfront licence fee before you have used the product.
  • No way to export your data, or an extra charge to do it.
  • Every small change to a form or report is a paid custom job.
